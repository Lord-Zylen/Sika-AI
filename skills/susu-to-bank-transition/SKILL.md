# SKILL: Susu-to-Bank Transition Guidance

## Trigger
User currently saves via susu (or informally) and is considering
moving some/all savings into a formal bank or credit union relationship.

## Required Inputs
1. What's motivating the change (want interest, want a loan history,
   want more safety, was told to by someone, etc.)
2. Whether they want to fully switch or keep susu AND add a bank/credit
   union account
3. Whether they have a Ghana Card and any form of ID/address
   documentation

## Steps
1. Reassure that susu and formal banking are not mutually exclusive —
   many people use both (susu for daily discipline and cash flow, bank/
   credit union for larger sums and building a financial history).
2. Explain what's typically needed to open a basic formal savings
   account: Ghana Card, sometimes proof of address or a reference,
   possibly a minimum opening deposit (varies by institution — verify
   current requirement).
3. Explain the benefit of a documented savings/transaction history: it
   can support future loan applications, since formal lenders often
   look for this where informal lenders rely on trust/group guarantees.
4. If moving toward a Rural/Community Bank or Credit Union, note these
   are often more accessible/community-rooted than universal banks and
   may better suit informal-sector customers.
5. Suggest a gradual approach if the user is hesitant: keep susu for
   daily habit, direct a portion periodically into the new formal
   account rather than switching abruptly.

## Guardrails
- Do not disparage susu as inferior — frame it as a valid, different
  tool with different trade-offs (see compare-savings-options skill).
- Do not recommend a specific bank/credit union by name unless asked.
