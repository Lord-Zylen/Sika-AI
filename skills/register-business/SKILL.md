# SKILL: Register a Business (Sole Proprietorship or Limited Company)

## Trigger
User wants to formalize/register a business (market stall, trade,
service, shop) in Ghana.

## Required Inputs (ask ONE at a time)
1. Business type/activity (trading, services, manufacturing, etc.)
2. Structure preference, if known: sole proprietor (individual,
   simplest) vs limited company (separate legal entity, more paperwork,
   needed for certain contracts/scaling); if unsure, explain the
   difference before asking them to choose
3. Whether they already have a Ghana Card (required for registration)

## Steps — Sole Proprietorship
1. Confirm they have a Ghana Card (this doubles as TIN under current
   integration).
2. Explain registration is done via the Registrar General's Department
   (RGD), including online options where available.
3. Outline general required steps: business name search/reservation,
   completing the registration form, paying the applicable fee,
   receiving the Business Registration Certificate.
4. Note they may separately need to register for GRA tax obligations
   (link to explain-tax-obligation skill) and, depending on the sector,
   a local Assembly business operating permit.

## Steps — Limited Liability Company
1. Explain additional requirements: proposed company name search,
   at least one director/shareholder, registered office address,
   company regulations/constitution.
2. Note this involves more documentation and cost than sole
   proprietorship and may benefit from a lawyer or registration agent
   for complex cases.
3. Point to RGD as the authoritative source for current forms/fees.

## Output Format
A numbered step list with a clear note on what's DONE BY THE USER
vs WHAT TO CONFIRM WITH RGD.

## Guardrails
- ALWAYS state that exact fees, forms, and processing times change and
  must be confirmed with the Registrar General's Department directly
  (or retrieved live if a tool is available) — do not quote specific
  fees from memory as current.
- Do not offer to register the business "for" the user — you are
  guiding, not executing.
