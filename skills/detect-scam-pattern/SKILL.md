# SKILL: Detect Scam Pattern (Protective Screening)

## Trigger
User describes a suspicious offer, message, call, or app — investment
scheme, loan app, MoMo message, job offer, "agent" contact — and wants
to know if it's a scam.

## Approach
Screen at the PATTERN level only. Never explain how a scam technique
is executed in a way that could be replicated — focus entirely on
recognition and protective action.

## Required Inputs
1. What happened (message received, call, app download, in-person
   approach) — let them describe freely
2. Was money, a PIN, an OTP, or personal ID information requested?
3. Was there urgency or pressure (e.g., "act now," "limited slots,"
   threat of account suspension)?

## Common Red-Flag Categories to Check Against (describe generally,
do not itemize as a step-by-step script)
- Requests for MoMo PIN, OTP, or full card details — legitimate
  institutions (banks, telcos, GRA) do not ask for these
- Guaranteed high/fast returns on "investment" with little or no risk
  explained
- Pressure to act immediately or keep the opportunity secret from
  family/friends
- Unlicensed lending apps demanding upfront fees before disbursing a
  loan
- Requests to send money first to "unlock" a prize, job, or refund
- Impersonation of known institutions (bank, MTN, GRA) via unofficial
  numbers or links

## Steps
1. Listen to what happened without judgment — do not make the user
   feel foolish.
2. Compare against the red-flag categories above generally (do not
   read out a numbered checklist that doubles as a how-to).
3. Give a clear verdict: "this shows signs consistent with a scam" /
   "this looks legitimate but verify directly" / "unclear — here's how
   to verify."
4. Recommend concrete protective action: never share PIN/OTP with
   anyone, verify through the official app/hotline/office directly
   (not a number given by the suspicious contact), and report to the
   telco fraud line or BoG/GRA if applicable.
5. If money was already sent or PINs shared, prioritize immediate
   action: contact the mobile money provider or bank fraud line right
   away, and consider reporting to the police.

## Guardrails
- Never name specific scam scripts, phrases used by scammers, or
  technical methods in detail — describe the pattern and the
  protective response only.
- Do not speculate on who is behind a specific scam.
- Always end with practical next steps, not just a diagnosis.
