# SKILL: Budget for Irregular/Seasonal Income

## Trigger
User has variable income (trader, farmer, driver, artisan, seasonal
worker) and wants help planning spending, saving, or surviving lean
periods.

## Required Inputs (ask ONE at a time)
1. Main income source(s) and roughly how often money comes in
   (daily/weekly/market-day/seasonal/harvest)
2. Rough income range: a "good" period vs a "slow" period (approximate
   is fine — do not demand precision)
3. Fixed unavoidable expenses (rent, school fees, data/airtime,
   transport) and their frequency
4. Any existing savings method (susu, MoMo, bank, "under the mattress,"
   none)
5. What they're trying to achieve (build emergency fund, save for a
   goal, survive the off-season, reduce debt)

## Steps
1. Establish a baseline "low income month" figure — budget against
   the WORST realistic period, not the average or best, since this is
   the standard approach for irregular earners.
2. Categorize expenses into: Must-Pay (rent, fees, food), Should-Pay
   (transport, data), Flexible (everything else).
3. Recommend a "smoothing" approach: during high-income periods, set
   aside a fixed portion (suggest starting at 10-20%, adjust to their
   comfort) into a separate savings channel (susu, MoMo goal wallet,
   or bank) that is NOT touched during good periods, to be drawn down
   only during lean periods.
4. If they have no savings method, walk through pros/cons of susu vs
   MoMo goal savings vs a savings account (see compare-savings-options
   skill) rather than picking one for them.
5. Build a simple two-column month plan: "Good Month" vs "Slow Month"
   showing income, must-pay, savings, and what's left.
6. Suggest reviewing the plan every 1-3 months, since informal income
   patterns shift with seasons.

## Output Format
A short simple table (Good Month / Slow Month) plus 2-3 sentences of
plain-language guidance. Avoid long lectures.

## Guardrails
- Do not assume the user is bad with money or imply judgment about
  past decisions.
- Do not push a specific savings product — present options.
- If the user reveals they cannot cover Must-Pay items even in a good
  month, do not simply say "spend less" — acknowledge the real
  constraint and focus on identifying any possible income or cost
  flexibility, and mention community/formal support options (e.g.,
  NHIS for health costs) if relevant.
