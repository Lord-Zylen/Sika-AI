BUSINESS REGISTRATION IN GHANA — SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP (REGISTRAR GENERAL'S DEPARTMENT / OFFICE OF THE REGISTRAR OF COMPANIES)

Business registration in Ghana is handled through the Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC), operating under the Registrar-General's Department (RGD). The RGD was originally established in 1950 and has long been the government body responsible for registering companies and businesses.

Steps to register a sole proprietorship ("business name" / "one-man business"):

1. Choose a unique business name and conduct a name search at RGD (in person or via the RGD portal, rgd.gov.gh) to confirm no one else is using it.
2. Apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) from the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) before registering the business — a copy of the Ghana Card is typically required for this. TIN issuance generally takes one to two days once submitted.
3. Complete the sole proprietorship registration form, known as Form A, available from RGD's website or offices. Required information typically includes: business name, business address (digital address, house/building details, street, city, district), postal address, email, phone number, and the residential address of the business owner.
4. Pay the applicable registration fee at an RGD-designated bank; you will receive an official receipt.
5. RGD issues a Certificate of Registration and a certified true copy of Form A once processing is complete. Registration commonly takes about 7 days, though expedited/"prestige" services may be available at some offices for faster turnaround.
6. Sole proprietorship business name registration must be renewed annually.

For registered companies (limited liability): additional requirements apply, including a proposed company name search, at least one director/shareholder, a registered office address, and company regulations/constitution documents. Company registration involves more documentation, higher fees (which vary based on authorised share capital), and may benefit from professional (legal/accounting) support.

Applications for both business types can generally be submitted online through the e-Registrar portal or in person at RGD/ORC offices.

Tax note: a sole proprietor and their sole proprietorship business are treated as one entity for tax purposes — the owner is personally liable for business debts, and business income is taxed as part of the owner's personal income.
