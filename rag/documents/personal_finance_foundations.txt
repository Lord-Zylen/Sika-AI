PERSONAL FINANCE FOUNDATIONS FOR GHANAIAN HOUSEHOLDS

Practical money-management basics, adapted to the realities of the Ghanaian economy: irregular income, a mix of cash and mobile money, large family obligations, and limited access to formal credit. These are general principles, not binding rules.

BUDGETING WITH IRREGULAR OR SEASONAL INCOME:
- When income varies (traders, farmers, drivers, artisans), do not budget from last month's peak. Use a "base income" — your most reliable, lowest realistic monthly income — and treat anything above it as surplus.
- Divide money into four buckets and decide shares up front:
  1. Essentials: rent, food, transport, school fees, health.
  2. Savings & investment: fixed amount or share of every sale — pay yourself first.
  3. Debt repayment: if you have loans, clear the most expensive first.
  4. Optional/fun: whatever remains.
- Use a simple tracker (notebook or a mobile wallet statement) so money doesn't silently disappear.
- During good months, pre-fund future essentials (buy staple food in bulk, top up school fees) to smooth bad months.

EMERGENCY FUND:
- Aim to build an emergency buffer equal to 1–3 months of essential expenses before taking on non-essential debt or investing.
- Keep it accessible: a dedicated mobile-money wallet or savings account that you don't touch except for real emergencies.
- Do not invest money you might need quickly in locked instruments (e.g., 364-day treasury bills) unless you can afford the lock-up.

SAVING AND INVESTING:
- Savings should earn something. Compare: susu (convenient, group discipline, but usually no interest), MoMo goal savings (some pay interest), bank fixed deposits, treasury bills, and credit-union savings. See the compare-savings-options skill.
- Only consider investments (stocks, bonds, funds) for money you can leave untouched for 1–3 years.
- If an offer promises "guaranteed" high returns, it is almost certainly a scam. Verify any institution with its regulator (BoG, SEC, NIC).

DEBT DISCIPLINE:
- A common safe guideline: total monthly debt payments should stay well below half of your base income.
- Never borrow to gamble or to buy things that lose value on credit at very high cost.
- If you are struggling, talk to the lender early — negotiating beats defaulting, which damages your credit file (see loans document).

FAMILY AND COMMUNITY MONEY:
- Set clear expectations with family/friends about what you can contribute — many Ghanaians over-give and under-save.
- Community savings (susu, VSLA, rotating groups) work best as discipline tools and emergency pools, not as the primary home for large savings.
- Protect against big shocks with insurance where cheap (e.g., NHIS, micro-insurance, compulsory motor third-party) rather than only relying on friends.

INFORMAL SECTOR AND FORMAL ACCOUNTS:
- Keep some savings in a formal account (bank or SDI) to build a documented history that can support future loans and to benefit from deposit protection up to the GDPC limit.
- You do not have to abandon susu — many people use both: susu for daily discipline, a bank/SDI for larger sums.
