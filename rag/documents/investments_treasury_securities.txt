INVESTING IN GHANA — TREASURY BILLS, NOTES AND BONDS

Treasury bills are short-term Government of Ghana securities issued through weekly auctions run by the Bank of Ghana. They are considered low-risk ("risk-free" in the sense of being backed by government) and are a common first investment for Ghanaians. Returns are known at purchase, which makes them predictable but modest.

TYPES OF INSTRUMENT:
- 91-day Treasury Bill (3 months)
- 182-day Treasury Bill (6 months)
- 364-day Treasury Bill (1 year)
- 1- and 2-year Treasury Notes; 3- and 5-year Treasury Bonds (longer tenors, sold less frequently)

HOW IT WORKS:
- Bills are sold at a discount: you pay less than face value and receive the full face value at maturity. The difference is your return.
- Interest on Government of Ghana securities is generally EXEMPT from tax for resident individuals.
- Rates are set at weekly auctions and change constantly. Indicative auction results (Bank of Ghana, early August 2026): 91-day bill approx. 5.76%, 182-day approx. 7.64%, 364-day approx. 12.98%. These are illustrative only — always check the latest BoG auction result (bog.gov.gh) or your bank's current quote before acting.

HOW TO BUY:
- Bank branches (e.g., GCB, Ecobank, Stanbic, etc.) — minimum typically around GHS 500, needs valid photo ID (Ghana Card recommended) and a bank account.
- Bank mobile apps — requires a CSD (Central Securities Depository) account registered first at a branch.
- Brokers/investment houses (e.g., Databank) — minimum approx. GHS 500; two passport photos + valid ID.
- Mobile money (e.g., Ecobank TBILL4ALL on MTN MoMo) — from as little as GHS 5 for 91/182-day bills, full payout to your MoMo wallet at maturity, with optional automatic reinvestment.
- BoG's official direct minimum is GHS 100, but individual banks/brokers set their own higher practical minimums — ask before planning around a floor.

TIPS:
- Longer tenors generally pay higher rates, but rates reset every auction and your money is locked for the term.
- A ladder (splitting across 91/182/364-day bills) gives regular maturities and liquidity.
- Set a standing instruction to auto-roll matured bills into the next auction to avoid idle cash.
- Treasury bills can also be used as collateral and their statements help with visa applications.

RISKS AND LIMITS:
- Purchasing power risk: if inflation exceeds your return, real returns can be negative. Compare against the current inflation rate.
- These are savings-grade investments, not high-growth investments. They are not bank deposits and are not covered by deposit insurance (GDPC) — though as government securities the default risk is considered very low.
- Money needed before maturity is locked in; early exit usually means selling on the secondary market, often at a discount.
